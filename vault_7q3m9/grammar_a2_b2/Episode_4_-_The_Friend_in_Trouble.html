The Friend in Trouble

Hasan and Robin study in the same university department. They usually sit together in class, share notes, and remind each other about assignments. Robin is the kind of friend who makes people laugh, but he is also serious about his goals. At least, he used to be. During the last few weeks, Hasan has noticed a change in him. Robin is missing deadlines, coming late to class, and spending long hours on online games and random videos.

One afternoon, Hasan saw Robin sitting in the cafeteria while the other students were preparing for a quiz. Robin was watching a video on his phone. Hasan sat beside him and asked, "Have you revised for tomorrow's quiz?" Robin waved. "Not yet. I'll do it at night." Hasan looked at the notebook that Robin had left unopened on the table. "You said the same thing yesterday," he replied gently.

Robin frowned. "Are you checking on me now?" Hasan felt uncomfortable, but he stayed calm. "No, I am not judging you. I am worried because you have missed two deadlines this week. If you continue like this, you will create more stress for yourself." Robin looked away and said, "I know what I'm doing." For a moment, Hasan wanted to stop talking, but he remembered that a supportive friend should speak with care, not anger.

Later that evening, Hasan sent Robin a short message: "I'm sorry if I sounded harsh. I only want to help." Robin did not reply immediately. The next morning, he came to class late again. Their teacher reminded everyone about a project that would be submitted on Sunday. After class, Robin looked tired. "I haven't started the project," he admitted quietly. "I have wasted too much time."

Hasan listened without making fun of him. "We can still fix some things," he said. "You should make a simple plan, and I can help you organise the work." Robin sighed. "I feel overwhelmed. Every time I open my books, I think about all the tasks I have ignored." Hasan nodded. "That happens. But one small step is better than avoiding everything."

They sat in the library, which was quiet and bright in the afternoon. Hasan took a blank page and wrote three headings: urgent tasks, study time, and rest time. "First, choose the deadline that is closest," he said. "Then we will divide the project into small parts." Robin looked at the plan and said, "This seems possible." Hasan smiled. "It is possible if you follow it honestly. I'm going to revise my own lesson now, and you are going to write the first paragraph of your project."

For the next hour, they worked silently. Robin checked his phone twice, but he put it back inside his bag. "I am trying," he whispered. Hasan replied, "That is already progress." By sunset, Robin had finished the first paragraph and collected two sources. He had not completed everything, but he had started. For the first time in many days, his face looked lighter.

On the way home, Robin said, "I became defensive because I felt ashamed. You were right to speak to me." Hasan shook his head. "I also learned something. Advice should be given with respect, or people may not accept it." Robin smiled. "From tomorrow, I will study in the library after class. Will you remind me if I forget?" Hasan laughed. "I can remind you, but you must take responsibility too."

By the end of the week, Robin had submitted his project and reduced his distractions. He was not perfect, and Hasan did not expect him to be. But Robin had begun making positive changes. Hasan felt grateful because their friendship had become stronger. He understood that a real friend is someone who encourages good choices without making others feel small.
