The Angry Message

Hasan was working on a group assignment at his college when his phone suddenly buzzed. The project was important because it would be counted for their final marks. Every evening, updates are posted in the class group, and documents are shared there by the team leader. Hasan usually checks the messages calmly, but that night he was tired and worried about the deadline.

A message from Rafiq appeared on the screen: "Hasan, you have not done your part properly. We cannot wait for you forever." Hasan stared at the words. His face became hot, and he felt that the message was rude and unfair.

"What does he mean?" Hasan said to himself. "I have already finished half of the slides."

He opened the chat and started typing a harsh reply. "You should check your own work before blaming me," he wrote. Then he added, "If you spoke more politely, people would respect you more." His thumb moved toward the send button.

At that moment, his younger brother entered the room. "Why are you typing so fast?" he asked.

"Rafiq has sent an angry message," Hasan said. "I am going to reply now."

His brother looked at the screen and said, "Maybe you should wait. Messages can be misunderstood. If you send this now, the problem will become bigger."

Hasan took a deep breath. He did not send the message. Instead, he saved the file, put the phone beside his books, and decided to talk the next morning. It was not easy. He was still annoyed, but he knew that an angry reaction could damage the group.

The next day, Hasan met Rafiq near the library. Rafiq looked embarrassed. "Hasan, I need to explain something," he said. "Yesterday I was under pressure. The message was written in a hurry, and I did not choose my words carefully. I did not mean to disrespect you."

Hasan felt calmer. "I was upset when I read it," he replied, "but I did not want to fight in the group chat. We should clarify problems before we judge each other."

Rafiq nodded. "You are right. I have also learned that short messages can sound harsher than we intend. The part which I asked about was only one slide. I thought it was missing."

Hasan opened his laptop and showed the slide that he had prepared the night before. "Here it is. I am going to add the references today. Then we can combine everything."

Soon the other teammates joined them. They had a short discussion in which everyone spoke respectfully. Tasks were divided again, and a new deadline was set. Rafiq apologized to the group, and Hasan rewrote his response in a calm way: "Thanks for explaining. I understand the pressure. Let us finish the project together."

By Thursday, the presentation had been completed. The slides were checked, the references were added, and the final file was uploaded before the deadline. Hasan felt relieved. He thought about the message he had almost sent. If he had sent it, the group might have argued for days.

That evening, Hasan wrote in his notebook, "A calm reply can solve a problem, but an angry reply can create a bigger one." He had learned that patience is not weakness. It is a skill that helps people communicate with respect and make better decisions.
