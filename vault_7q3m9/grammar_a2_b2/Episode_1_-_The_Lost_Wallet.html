The Lost Wallet

Hasan studies English at a private university in Sylhet. He usually goes home by bus after class, but that afternoon the sky was dark and the road was crowded. He was walking quickly past a small tea stall when he saw a black wallet near the footpath. At first, he thought it was only an empty cover. Then he picked it up and felt its weight.

"Maybe someone has just dropped it," Hasan said to himself. He looked around. People were getting on buses, a rickshaw driver was calling for passengers, and shopkeepers were closing their stalls because rain was coming. No one seemed to be searching for anything.

Hasan opened the wallet carefully. Inside, there was a good amount of money, a university ID card, a national ID photocopy, and some important papers. The name on the university card was Arif Hossain. Hasan had never met him, but he understood one thing clearly: this wallet was not a small matter. Someone might be very worried at that moment.

For a few seconds, Hasan stood silently. He had been saving money for a new phone, and his old phone was almost broken. A small thought came into his mind: "If I keep the money, I can solve my problem." He felt uncomfortable as soon as he thought that. He remembered what his mother often says: "A trusted person protects what does not belong to him." He also remembered that honesty is not only a word; it is a choice when nobody is watching.

Hasan closed the wallet and took a deep breath. "No," he whispered. "I must return it." He went back to the tea stall and asked the owner, "Uncle, has anyone come here looking for a wallet?" The man shook his head. "Not yet. But you should leave your number with me. If someone comes, I will tell him." Hasan gave his phone number, but he did not leave the wallet there. The documents were important, and he felt responsible for keeping them safe.

Then Hasan checked the university ID again. It showed the department and a student number. "I am going to call the university office," he decided. He searched online for the office number and called. A staff member answered, "We can try to contact the student, but it may take some time." Hasan replied, "I have found his wallet. Please tell him that it is safe."

While Hasan was waiting near the bus stop, his phone rang. A nervous voice said, "Take my Salam! Brother, are you Hasan? I am Arif. Have you really found my wallet?" Hasan smiled and answered, "Peace be upon you! Yes, I have it. Your ID card and documents are inside. Where are you now?" Arif sounded relieved. "I am coming back from the campus. I have been looking for it for almost an hour. My exam admit card is inside, and I thought I had lost everything."

Hasan waited under a shop shade as the rain began. Twenty minutes later, Arif arrived, breathing fast and looking worried. Hasan asked him to describe the wallet before giving it back. Arif described it correctly, and Hasan handed it to him. Arif opened it, checked the documents, and almost laughed with relief. "Brother, you have saved me from a huge problem. I don't know how to thank you," he said.

Hasan replied, "You do not need to thank me too much. Anyone can lose something. If I lose my wallet one day, I hope someone will return it too." Arif offered him some money as a reward, but Hasan gently refused. "Please keep me in your prayer. That is enough," he said.

On the bus home, Hasan felt light and peaceful. His phone was still broken, and he still needed money to buy a new one. But his heart was calm. He had chosen honesty when he could have chosen the easy way. He knew he would remember this day for a long time, not because he found a wallet, but because he returned a trust to its owner.
