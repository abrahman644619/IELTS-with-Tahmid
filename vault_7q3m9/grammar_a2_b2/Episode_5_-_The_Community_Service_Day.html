The Community Service Day

Hasan studies at a college in Sylhet, and he usually keeps himself busy with classes, assignments, and family responsibilities. One Monday morning, his English teacher wrote a notice on the board: Community Service Day will be held this Thursday. All students can join a project that will help the local area. Hasan read the notice twice because he had never joined a college volunteer project before.

During the break, some students were talking excitedly near the classroom door. "We are going to clean the small public reading corner beside the market," said Rafi. "We will also organize books and learning materials for younger students who visit the centre." Another student laughed and said, "It sounds like hard work. I think it will be a waste of time." Hasan did not answer at first. He understood that the day might be tiring, but he also felt that students should contribute when they can.

On Thursday morning, Hasan arrived with a group of volunteers. The reading corner looked dusty, and many books were lying in different boxes. Some posters were old, and a few notebooks had no covers. For a moment, the task seemed too big. "How will we finish all this before afternoon?" one student asked. Their teacher smiled and said, "If you work together, you will finish more than you expect."

Hasan started by making a simple plan. "Rafi, you can check the books. Sumi, you can make labels. I will arrange the tables with two others," he said. At first, everyone moved slowly, but soon the team found a rhythm. Some students were sweeping the floor, some were sorting books, and others were writing names on small labels. A few students who had been quiet in class showed strong leadership during the project.

After an hour, the place began to change. The shelves looked neat, the tables were clean, and the learning materials were easier to find. A young boy came with his little sister and looked at the books. "Can we read here?" he asked. Hasan smiled and replied, "Yes, you can. This space is for students like you." The boy's face became bright, and Hasan felt that their effort had already made a difference.

Later, the team packed extra notebooks and pencils into small sets for younger learners. One student, who had called the project a waste of time, quietly said, "I was wrong. I have realized that small actions can help many people." Hasan nodded and said, "Teamwork makes difficult work lighter. We should do this again next month."

By the end of the day, everyone was tired, but nobody complained. They had organized the books, cleaned the space, and prepared learning materials for the children. Their teacher thanked them and said, "You have shown responsibility today. Real leadership is not only about giving instructions. It is also about serving others." Hasan walked home slowly, feeling proud and peaceful. He knew that the project had not solved every problem in the community, but it had taught him something important: when people work together with kindness, even a small effort can become a meaningful achievement.
