The Missing Alarm

Hasan usually wakes up before sunrise. He keeps his phone beside his bed, drinks a glass of water, reviews his plan for the day, and then gets ready for class. His routine is not perfect, but it helps him feel calm. On Wednesday night, however, he was tired after a long day at university. He had a presentation in the morning, and he was going to meet his group at 7:30 a.m. to practise one last time.

Before sleeping, Hasan set one alarm for 6:00 a.m. "This is enough," he said to himself. His younger sister, Lina, was arranging her books on the next table. She looked at him and asked, "Are you sure? Your phone battery is only twelve percent." Hasan smiled. "Don't worry. I can wake up easily." He placed the phone near his pillow, but he did not connect the charger. He also kept scrolling for a few more minutes, and those few minutes became half an hour.

The next morning, sunlight was already entering the room when Hasan opened his eyes. For a second, he did not understand what was happening. Then he saw the clock: 7:15 a.m. His phone was dead. The alarm had not rung. Hasan jumped out of bed and felt his heart beating fast. "Oh no! I have missed the alarm," he said. He washed his face quickly and looked for his presentation file. His notes were on the desk, but his bag was not ready.

At first, Hasan blamed the phone. "This alarm is useless," he said angrily. Lina stood at the door and replied softly, "The alarm was not the only problem. You were not ready before sleeping." Hasan wanted to argue, but he knew she was right. If he continued like this, he would face the same problem again. He reached the campus late, and his group had already started practising without him. "I'm sorry," Hasan told them. "I should have prepared better last night."

After class, Hasan felt disappointed. His teacher did not scold him, but she said, "Responsibility begins before the important moment. Small habits protect big goals." These words stayed in Hasan's mind. In the evening, he sat with his uncle, who often gave him practical advice. Hasan explained everything. His uncle listened carefully and said, "You cannot control every problem, but you can reduce many problems with a clear routine. Tonight, you should prepare your bag, charge your phone, sleep earlier, and set two reminders. If you prepare before sleeping, you will wake up with less stress."

Hasan nodded. "I have been careless with my nights," he said. "I am going to change it from today." He made a simple checklist: charge the phone, keep the notebook inside the bag, put clothes on the chair, set two alarms, and stop using the phone after 10:30 p.m. He also wrote one sentence at the top of the page: Good habits make hard days easier.

That night, Hasan followed the checklist. He was preparing his bag when Lina walked past the room. "Another presentation?" she asked. Hasan laughed. "No, but I am practising discipline." He set one alarm on his phone and another on a small clock. He placed both across the room so that he would have to stand up to turn them off. Then he slept earlier than usual.

The next morning, the clock rang loudly at 6:00 a.m. Hasan got up, turned it off, and smiled. His phone alarm rang two minutes later. This time, he was ready. His bag was packed, his clothes were prepared, and his mind felt lighter. He did not become a perfect person in one day, but he had taken a real step. As he walked to campus on time, he felt proud because he had accepted his mistake and improved his habit.
