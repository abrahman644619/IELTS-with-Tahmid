The Exam Stress Week

Hasan is a second-year university student in Sylhet. He studies English and Business, and he usually tries to stay organized. But during one exam week, everything felt heavy. He had three subjects to revise, two assignments to submit, and a presentation to prepare. Every morning he opened his books, but after a few minutes he checked his phone. Then he watched short videos, replied to messages, and told himself, "I will start after ten minutes."

The ten minutes became one hour. By Wednesday evening, Hasan was sitting at his desk with six open notebooks around him. His mother called from the kitchen, "Hasan, are you studying?" He answered, "I am trying, mom, but I cannot focus." In truth, he was reading the same paragraph again and again. His mind was thinking about marks, failure, and what other students were doing.

His exam was coming next Monday, and Hasan felt overwhelmed. He had not revised the accounting chapters, and he had not finished the English notes. "I have wasted two days," he whispered. "If I continue like this, I will not be ready." He closed his book and put his head on the desk. For a moment, he only wanted to sleep and forget everything.

That evening, after evening prayer, Hasan sat with his father on the balcony. His father noticed his tired face and asked, "What is happening, Hasan?" Hasan said, "Dear dad, I am studying, but I am not improving. I am always worrying. Everyone seems prepared, but I am behind." His father listened quietly. Then he said, "Worry is not a study plan. You should make a small plan and follow it step by step. Do your part, make prayer, and trust Allah (God) with the result."

Hasan looked down and said, "But what if I cannot finish everything?" His father replied, "If you divide the work, you will see it clearly. You can revise the important topics first. You must sleep enough, and you should keep your phone away while studying. 'Trust in God' does not mean sitting without effort. It means working sincerely and then leaving the result to Allah (God)."

The advice was simple, but it felt practical. Hasan took a notebook and created a schedule. From 8:00 to 9:00, he was going to revise accounting formulas. From 9:15 to 10:00, he would review English grammar notes. After a short break, he planned to practise past questions. He also wrote a small reminder at the top of the page: "Effort first, then trust in Allah (God)."

The next morning, Hasan put his phone in another room. At first, he felt restless because he wanted to check notifications. But he reminded himself, "I can check later. Now I must focus." He studied for forty minutes without stopping. It was not perfect, but it was progress. After every study block, he ticked one task on his schedule. The small ticks gave him confidence.

On Friday, Hasan met his classmate Sami at the campus library. Sami asked, "Are you still stressed?" Hasan smiled a little. "Yes, but I am managing it better. I have finished two chapters, and I have started revising the difficult parts." Sami said, "I am also worried. Can we study together for one hour?" Hasan agreed. They asked each other questions, corrected mistakes, and shared short notes.

By Sunday night, Hasan had not become a perfect student. He still felt nervous, and some topics were still difficult. But he was calmer than before. He had followed his schedule, reduced distractions, and done his best. Before sleeping, he made dua and prepared his admit card, pens, and calculator. He said to himself, "Tomorrow I will try my best. If I stay calm and manage my time, I can answer better."

Hasan went to bed early that night. The result was still unknown, but his heart felt lighter. He had learned that patience is not waiting without action. It is continuing with discipline when the work feels hard. He had also learned that trusting Allah (God) gives strength, especially after honest effort.
