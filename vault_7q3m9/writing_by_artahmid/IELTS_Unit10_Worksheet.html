Model Text 1 — পড়ো ও বিশ্লেষণ করো

Essay Topic:

Some parents believe that there is no harm in allowing their children to watch TV programmes and movies that contain a lot of violence. To what extent do you agree with this opinion?

Model 1 — Two Body Paragraphs

Some people think that if children watch violent images on television, they may try to copy the things they see. As a result, they could behave more violently towards other people. While it is sometimes true that children love to copy some of the violence they see in the games they play, they generally know the difference between real and imaginary violence. For example, some children love kung fu movies and television shows. They often like to play at fighting each other, and they copy the scenes they watch on TV. Yet, most of these children do not grow up to be violent.

Another argument against allowing children to watch violent TV programmes is that these programmes could make them frightened or give them nightmares. However, there is a difference between imaginary violence, such as cartoons, and real violence, such as police dramas or the TV news. For example, most children might laugh at the violence in a funny cartoon, but be disturbed by seeing a child being beaten in a news report. For this reason, parents need to consider what is appropriate for their own child and select programmes carefully.

Grammar Focus 1 | Qualifying Statements — Modal Verbs
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট

Academic writing-এ আমরা সবসময় ১০০% নিশ্চিত বলি না। Modal verbs ব্যবহার করে আমরা statement-কে 'soften' করি — এটা IELTS Task 2-এ বেশি academic শোনায়।

Key modal verbs: could / may / can / might + base verb
  • could — পারে / হতে পারে। সবচেয়ে কম নিশ্চিত। → Children could be disturbed by violent images.
  • may — সম্ভব। কম নিশ্চিত। → Violent images may cause children to have nightmares.
  • can — সম্ভব / সাধারণত পারে। → Exposure to violent scenes can negatively influence behaviour.
  • might — পারে। 'could'-এর মতো কম নিশ্চিত। → Children might copy what they see on TV.
Important: Modals are ALWAYS followed by a BASE FORM verb — NEVER add -s, -ing, or -ed.

Correct: This may affect children.

Wrong: This may affects / may affecting children.

9B — Read Model 2 | Model 2 পড়ো ও মিলিয়ে দেখো
Model 2

Generally speaking, software programs, such as Net Nanny, do provide some protection from violent or sex sites for children. For example, if children find inappropriate websites, the Net Nanny program will usually not allow them to open these pages. As a result, children are mostly safe when they use the net with these programs. However, occasionally, children may be able to open some sites that are not suitable for them to see even though they have a software censorship program.

There is a difference between a software program and the loving care and control of a parent. Parents are responsible for limiting and checking what their children see on the Internet, and they are generally the best people to guide children from day to day. In particular, they mostly know which sites may be harmful to their children. For this reason, it is their responsibility to watch and limit the sites that their children can access. The Internet is a powerful tool, and a software program by itself might not be sufficient to protect children from inappropriate influences.

Teacher Notes  |  Unit 10 — Censorship: Teaching Tips
  • This unit introduces two key IELTS Task 2 writing skills simultaneously: qualifying statements (modals + adverbs) and comprehensive linking word use. Treat these as distinct skills before combining.
  • Section 3 (Warm-Up): The 'two-sided essay' question (Q4) is important — many students think an opinion essay means writing only one view. Clarify: in a two-sided essay, you acknowledge the other side AND give your own view. Model 1 demonstrates this clearly.
  • Section 4A (Model 1 Analysis): Focus on how the writer uses 'While..., they generally...' structure. This is the 'acknowledge + counter' pattern. Write the structure on the board: 'While it is [SOMETIMES] true that [OTHER SIDE], [MAIN POINT]'.
  • Section 6 (Modal Verbs): Students often overuse 'will' in Task 2 essays. Emphasise: 'will' sounds too certain and too absolute for academic writing. Replace with 'could/may/might' for appropriate hedging.
  • Section 7 (Qualifying Adverbs): Placement is the key difficulty. Sentence adverbs (Generally speaking, On the whole) go at the BEGINNING. Mid-position adverbs (often, frequently, sometimes) go BEFORE the main verb. Model this explicitly.
  • Section 8 (Linking Words): Do Exercise 8A as a whole-class activity first. Put the function headings on the board and ask students to place words under them. Common errors: confusing 'Furthermore' (adding) with 'However' (contrasting); overusing 'Moreover' from Bengali translations.
  • Section 8C (Gap-Fill): Multiple correct answers are possible. Mark on appropriateness of function, not exact match. Use the discussion to explore WHY certain words fit better than others.
  • Section 9A (Planning + Own Paragraphs): Insist students write their own version BEFORE looking at Model 2. Comparing after attempting is pedagogically stronger than just reading the model.
  • Section 10 (Writing Practice): Use the self-check as a marking rubric. A good paragraph should show evidence of: hedging (modals), qualification (adverbs), balanced argument, varied linking words.
