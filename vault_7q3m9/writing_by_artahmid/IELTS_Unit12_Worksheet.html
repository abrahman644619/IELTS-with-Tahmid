শেখার লক্ষ্য | Learning Objectives

এই worksheet শেষ করলে তুমি পারবে —

  • Read and interpret data from a pie chart showing percentages.
  • Use correct verbs with sports (do/play) and leisure activities (go, go to, listen to, chat on).
  • Use 'per cent' (with numbers) and 'percentage' (with comparatives) correctly.
  • Use 'as a percentage of' in the topic sentence of a pie chart description.
  • Use ranking superlatives with ordinal numbers: the most popular / the second most popular / the third most popular / the second least popular / the least popular.
  • Use data description verbs: show, make up, share, attract.
  • Use qualifying phrases: with [X]% participation / at [X]% / with [X] per cent of students involved.
  • Vary sentence structure to avoid repetition in a pie chart description.
  • Write a full pie chart description paragraph using all the above skills.

5C — Word Forms: 'per cent' vs 'percentage' | 'per cent' না 'percentage'?
Grammar Rule | ব্যাকরণ নিয়ম

per cent → সংখ্যার সাথে ব্যবহার হয়। → Ex: 30 per cent / 25 per cent / 12.5 per cent / 65% of students

percentage → comparative words বা adjective-এর সাথে ব্যবহার হয়। → Ex: a larger percentage / the highest percentage / the same percentage / what percentage?

Fill in 'per cent' or 'percentage' in the rule below. Then complete the examples.

In English, we use the word with comparative words. For example, we say, 'A larger of students prefer soccer to basketball.' or, 'The highest of students do drama.' We use with numbers. For example, we say, '65 of students prefer soccer, and 35 prefer basketball.'

Grammar Focus | Ranking Superlatives
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট

Pie chart-এর সেগমেন্টগুলো rank করতে ordinal numbers ও superlatives ব্যবহার করো:

  • the most popular (১ম) → the most popular sport is soccer
  • the second most popular (২য়) → basketball is the second most popular sport
  • the third most popular (৩য়) → tennis is the third most popular sport
  • the second least popular (২য় সর্বনিম্ন) → volleyball and swimming are the second least popular
  • the least popular (সর্বনিম্ন) → the least popular sport is athletics

Pattern: [Subject] is the [ordinal + most/least popular] [type], with [X]% participation.

Also useful: This is followed by [subject], with [X]% participation.

7A — Using 'as a percentage of' | 'as a percentage of' ব্যবহার করো
Pattern for pie chart topic sentences:

The pie chart shows [what is shown] AS A PERCENTAGE OF [the whole].

Example: The pie chart shows the six sporting activities of male students at Hamilton University as a percentage of total participation.

Rule: 'the part' comes first, 'the whole' comes after 'as a percentage of'.

  • with [X]% participation → Tennis is third, with 15% participation.

Language Focus | Verbs for Describing Pie Chart Data
Key verbs: show / make up / share / attract
  • show: The pie chart SHOWS the sporting activities of male students. (এটি data describe করে।)
  • make up: Team sports MAKE UP over two thirds of all sports. (একাধিক অংশ একসাথে সম্পূর্ণটা তৈরি করে।)
  • share: Volleyball and swimming SHARE equal popularity. (দুটো item-এর rate একই।)
  • attract: Athletics ATTRACTS only 5 per cent of students. (কতজন কোনো activity-তে আকৃষ্ট হয়।)

Language Focus | Varying Sentence Structure

Why vary sentence structure?
Look at this paragraph. Every sentence is correct, but it would get a POOR IELTS score. Why?

Soccer is the most popular sport, at 25%. Volleyball is the second most popular sport, at 20%. Hockey is the third most popular sport, at 18%. Tennis is the fourth most popular sport, at 15%.

Every sentence has EXACTLY the same structure. This is boring and shows low writing skill. Vary your sentences using:

  • Different sentence openers (start with the subject / start with 'This is followed by...' / start with 'The least popular...')
  • Different verbs (show / make up / share / attract / is followed by)
  • Different qualifying phrase positions (with at the end vs at the beginning)

Teacher Notes

Unit 12 — Leisure Activities: Teaching Tips

  • This worksheet includes 3 HD pie charts: Charts 1 (sports/Model 1), 2 (subjects/Model 2 guided), 3 (girls' leisure/independent practice).
  • Section 3 (Warm-Up): Q3 on verb selection (do/play/go) is diagnostic. If students cannot correctly categorise activities, spend extra time on Section 5A–5B before proceeding.
  • Section 5A–5B (Verb Tables): The key rules are: DO → individual activities without a ball (athletics, swimming, drama, painting); PLAY → sports with a ball or team sports; GO → activities expressed as gerunds (dancing, swimming, shopping); GO TO → venues (cinema, nightclubs). There is some overlap — accept reasonable answers.
  • Section 5C (Per cent vs Percentage): Drill this rule explicitly. The most common IELTS error is '30 percentage' or 'a large per cent'. Write on the board: PER CENT → after a NUMBER. PERCENTAGE → after a COMPARATIVE (larger, smaller, highest, etc.).
  • Section 6 (Ranking Superlatives): Do the first ranking sentence together as a class, then ask students to continue independently. Insist on ordinal numbers — 'second most popular', NOT 'more popular than the third'.
  • Section 7 (Qualifying Phrases): Students often forget to add qualifying phrases, leaving sentences like 'Soccer is the most popular sport.' Practise adding 'with X%...' or 'at X%' to every data sentence.
  • Section 9 (Sentence Variety): The 'boring paragraph' example on page 109 of the book is excellent. Show students this paragraph and ask: 'Would you give this a high score? Why not?' This motivates varied sentence structure.
  • Section 10B (Guided Writing): Insist students write FIRST before reading Model 2. This is a critical pedagogical sequence. Students who read the model first do not develop independent writing skills.
  • Section 11 (Writing Practice): Chart 3 has 7 segments. This means 7 data points to describe in one paragraph. Remind students: start with topic sentence → overall → rank from most to least. They should NOT describe in the chart order.
  • Islamic leisure note: This unit invites brief discussion of halal and appropriate leisure activities. Islam encourages balance (wasatiyyah) in recreation — physical activity, social time, and intellectual pursuits are all valued. A brief mention can enrich the warm-up discussion.
