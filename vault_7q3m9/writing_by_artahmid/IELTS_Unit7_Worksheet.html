Essay Topic (Task 2 Question):

Some people think that single-sex schools are better for students than co-educational schools. To what extent do you agree with this opinion?

Model 1 gives ONE body paragraph — the writer's view that single-sex schools are better.

Model 1 — In favour of single-sex schools

Single-sex high schools help to produce better students because there are fewer distractions in these kinds of schools than in co-educational schools. Nowadays, students are learning more than ever before, and while they are trying to understand all this new knowledge, they do not need distractions. If boys and girls study together, they may become more focused on each other and not concentrate on the lesson. For example, a male student might be more interested in impressing a female peer than listening to the teacher; similarly, female students might get into arguments over popular boys or spend their time discussing the boys in their class instead of listening to the teacher. If high schools are single-sex, these kinds of distractions can be reduced.

Grammar Focus 1 | Present Simple vs Present Continuous
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট
PRESENT SIMPLE → সবসময় সত্য বা অভ্যাস (facts, habits, general truths):

→ Facts / general truths: Single-sex schools reduce distractions.

→ Habits / routines: Teachers usually help students when they have problems.

→ Stative verbs (love, like, hate, know, believe, prefer): I believe single-sex schools are better.

PRESENT CONTINUOUS → এখন হচ্ছে বা সাময়িক (around now / temporary / ongoing):

→ Continuing situation around now: Nowadays, students are learning more than ever before.

→ Temporary action (not permanent): While they are trying to understand new knowledge, they need focus.

→ Action in progress / not yet finished: My peers are working hard on the assignment.

Key rule:

Stative verbs (know, believe, like, love, hate, want, prefer, understand, mean) are NEVER used in continuous tense.

Grammar Focus 2 | First Conditional + Modal Hedging
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট
Structure:

If + subject + present simple verb …, subject + modal verb + base form verb …

Purpose:

Academic writing-এ future সম্পর্কে বলার সময় আমরা সবসময় 'will' ব্যবহার না করে modal verbs ব্যবহার করি — এটা আমাদের idea-কে কম নিশ্চিত ও বেশি academic করে।

  • will — সবচেয়ে নিশ্চিত। সাধারণত academic writing-এ এর চেয়ে কম নিশ্চিত শব্দ ভালো। → If students study, they WILL pass.
  • can — সম্ভব — ability। → If schools are single-sex, distractions CAN be reduced.
  • may — সম্ভব কিন্তু নিশ্চিত নয়। → If boys and girls study together, they MAY become distracted.
  • might — 'may'-এর চেয়ে কম নিশ্চিত। → A student MIGHT be more interested in impressing peers.
  • could — শর্তসাপেক্ষে সম্ভব। → If you work hard, you COULD get a high score.
Examples from Model 1:

→ If boys and girls study together, they MAY become more focused on each other…

→ If high schools are single-sex, these kinds of distractions CAN be reduced.

Language Focus | Paragraph Structure | Paragraph-এর গঠন
Basic Structure of an Opinion Paragraph | মতামত paragraph-এর গঠন
  • A. TOPIC SENTENCE — Give the main point/opinion of the paragraph. (প্রথমে মূল মত বলো।)
  • B. REASONS — Explain WHY you hold this opinion — give evidence or logic. (কেন এই মত — কারণ দাও।)
  • C. EXAMPLES — Give specific examples to support your reasons. (নির্দিষ্ট উদাহরণ দাও।)
Example from Model 1:

A (Topic): Single-sex high schools help to produce better students because there are fewer distractions…

B (Reason): Nowadays, students are learning more than ever before, and while they are trying to understand all this new knowledge, they do not need distractions.

C (Example): For example, a male student might be more interested in impressing a female peer than listening to the teacher…

Paragraph a | Topic: Team sports and social skills

Team members must pass the ball to each other, so they must learn to co-operate, and if they don't pass at the right time, their whole team can lose the game.
Doing team sports teaches about co-operation, and being a member of a team can build social skills.
Finally, team members often become good friends, and they build close relationships with each other while they are playing together.
In addition, members of a team must learn to play together even if they disagree or don't like each other very much.

Other health problems include eye strain, poor fitness, and even obesity because of the lack of exercise.

Paragraph c | Topic: Restricting car traffic in cities

For example, moving quickly to meetings is very important for businessmen, and if they have no car, this could create problems for them.

Section 4A — Model 1 Analysis
  1. Topic sentence: 'Single-sex high schools help to produce better students because there are fewer distractions in these kinds of schools than in co-educational schools.'
  2. Reason: Fewer distractions — students are learning more than ever before and need to concentrate on new knowledge; they do not need distractions.
  3. Examples: (i) A male student might be more interested in impressing a female peer than listening to the teacher; (ii) Female students might get into arguments over popular boys or spend their time discussing boys instead of listening.
  4. 'If' sentences: 'If boys and girls study together, they may become more focused on each other and not concentrate on the lesson.' / 'If high schools are single-sex, these kinds of distractions can be reduced.'
  5. Modals: may (become focused), might (be interested / get into arguments), can (be reduced).
  6. Present continuous: are learning / are trying.
  7. 'Can' not 'will': In academic writing we avoid claiming 100% certainty. 'Can be reduced' means it is possible / achievable, not guaranteed. 'Will be reduced' would sound too certain and less academic.

Section 9B — Model 2 Analysis
  1. 'If' sentences: 'If young people only interact with their same-sex peers, they may not feel comfortable in mixed company…' / 'If they are socialising with the opposite sex for the first time at university, they may be distracted…'
  2. Present continuous verbs: are learning / are socialising
  3. Topic sentence (A): 'On the other hand, co-ed schools have some advantages for teenagers because they offer the opportunity to learn important social skills.'
  4. Reason (B): Young people need to learn social skills and how to interact with the opposite sex before they go to university, or they may struggle.
  5. Example (C): 'For example, they may never learn how to talk easily to the opposite sex, and they might do some very foolish things when they first start to study with the opposite sex.'
Section 10 — Writing Practice (Model Paragraph — Teacher Reference)

Furthermore, co-ed schools can help students develop the social skills they will need in later life. If students only study with peers of the same sex, they may not know how to work effectively with the opposite sex when they enter the workplace. For example, young people are increasingly working in mixed-gender teams in business and professional settings, and if they have never interacted with the opposite sex at school, they might find this very difficult. In addition, co-ed schools can help students understand and respect the different perspectives that men and women bring to class discussions. If students are learning to appreciate these differences from an early age, they may become more tolerant and co-operative adults.

Teacher Notes

Unit 7 — Education: Schools: Teaching Tips

  • This is the first Task 2 unit that explicitly teaches the three-part paragraph structure (A-B-C: Topic → Reasons → Examples). Take time to teach this clearly before proceeding to grammar.
  • Section 3 (Warm-Up): Question 2 (single-sex vs co-ed) should be handled sensitively. For an Islamic school, this topic has direct real-world relevance. Encourage students to give academic reasons for their views, not just personal preferences.
  • Section 4A (Model 1 Analysis): Before students answer the questions, ask them to read the model once and underline the topic sentence themselves. Many students struggle to identify topic sentences — this deliberate practice is essential.
  • Section 6 (Present Simple vs Continuous): The key rule students must internalize is: present simple for FACTS; present continuous for IN PROGRESS actions. The stative verb rule is the most commonly broken — drill it explicitly.
  • Section 7 (First Conditional + Modal Hedging): Emphasise that academic writing avoids certainty. 'will' sounds too certain. 'may/might' sounds appropriately measured. This is a crucial IELTS Task 2 writing skill.
  • Section 8B (Paragraph Ordering): This exercise develops paragraph awareness — a key skill for both reading AND writing. After ordering, ask students to explain their choices: 'Why did you put this sentence here?'
  • Section 9 (Model 2 Gap-Fill): Do NOT reveal Model 2 until students have attempted the gap-fill. Even a wrong answer activates schema. The comparison with the model is more meaningful after attempting it.
  • Section 10 (Writing Practice): Use the A-B-C structure checklist as both a self-assessment tool AND a marking guide. Award marks for: (1) clear topic sentence, (2) at least one reason, (3) at least one example.
  • Total suggested class time: 70–85 minutes. Sections 6C, 7B, and 10 can be assigned as homework.
