Background Reading

Billions of people around the world use mobile phones every day. Mobile phones emit non-ionising electromagnetic radiation, and some people believe that this may be bad for our health and may cause cancer or brain damage. Many researchers all over the world have conducted studies to find out if this belief is true. These studies include both laboratory research and long-term studies of cell phone users. Some of these studies have followed many users over long periods of time. So far, none of these studies have been able to find any link between cell phone use and disease.

Essay Topic 1:

Some people say that mobile phones represent a danger to users because of the possible health issues, such as cancer or brain damage, associated with them. Others say that mobile phones are a great benefit to mankind.

Evaluate these two viewpoints and give your own opinion.

Model 1 — Mobile Phones: Two Viewpoints and Opinion

Mobile phones are becoming increasingly popular nowadays, and the number of people using them has grown hugely, especially in developing countries. There is some debate about the safety of mobile phones, but in my opinion, the benefits from these phones are far greater than any possible health risks.

There is some concern that mobile phones may cause brain damage or cancer because of the radiation they emit while they are being used. Some people are also worried that cell phone towers may be bad for health. However, researchers have conducted experiments over many years to find out if this is true, and so far, they have found no evidence for this.

On the other hand, mobile phones have many benefits, both for individuals and for isolated communities. They help individuals to communicate quickly and easily, and they can be used both for social reasons and in emergencies. For example, people can use them to contact their friends, and if they use texting, the phones are cheap and convenient. They are also very useful if someone has an accident while driving. In addition, isolated communities which do not have landlines can use mobile phones to contact the outside world, and this is much cheaper than installing landline phones.

In conclusion, although some people are worried about the health risks of cell phones, there is little or no evidence for this. However, there is a lot of evidence for the benefits of cell phones, so in my opinion, we should not worry about the dangers of cell phone use.

'While' + Present Continuous | 'while' + Present Continuous ব্যবহার
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট

'while' ব্যবহার হয় দুটি একযোগে ঘটমান কাজ যুক্ত করতে।

Continuing action → present continuous (V-ing) + another action → present simple

উদাহরণ: ...they are also very useful if someone has an accident while driving.
(Full form: while they are driving → omit subject + "be" in formal writing)

উদাহরণ: people can get directions while travelling to their destination.

7A — Rewrite Using 'while' | 'while' ব্যবহার করে পুনরায় লেখো

Combine each pair of ideas using 'while'. Do NOT include the subject or the verb 'be' after 'while'. The example shows you how. (প্রতিটি জোড়া idea-কে 'while' ব্যবহার করে একটি বাক্যে লেখো।)

Example | উদাহরণ

(drive) to work — many people (waste) time in traffic congestion

→ While driving to work, many people waste time in traffic congestion.

a. many people (have) accidents — (drive) to work
b. chatting on a cell phone (drive) — (be) dangerous
c. it (be) sensible to take precautions — (use) a cell phone
d. texting (drive) — (cause) accidents
e. people (expose) to radiation — (use) cell phones
f. talking (drive) — (lead to) loss of concentration
g. cell phones (emit) radiation — (use)
h. you should (avoid) drinking alcohol — (drive)

Model 2 — Cell Phones in Cars: Safety or Danger?

More and more people are using mobile phones nowadays, and the number of people using these phones in their cars has increased. There is some debate about the safety of mobile phone use in cars. Some people believe they make car travel safer, but others think they can cause accidents. This essay will evaluate these two ideas and give my opinion.

Mobile phones can make car travel safer because they allow people to quickly and easily contact emergency services if there is an accident. For example, if someone sees an accident while driving, they can call an ambulance and help can arrive very quickly. Similarly, if a driver is threatened by other drivers or by thieves, he can lock his car doors and contact the police without leaving his vehicle. In addition, mobile phones can help people to get directions if they get lost while travelling to their destination.

However, although mobile phones can increase safety, they should not be used to chat while driving. This is very dangerous because it can lead to loss of concentration and to car accidents. The three main causes of car accidents are careless driving, drinking alcohol, and talking while driving. Even a hands-free phone is harmful because the driver is not concentrating on the road while he is talking.

In conclusion, although mobile phones can make driving safer in some situations, we should not use them to talk to our friends or family while we are driving. This is very dangerous and causes many accidents every year.

Teacher Notes

Unit 19 — Technology: Communication: Teaching Tips & Notes

  • This is the first unit to combine three present tenses in one exercise (6B). Bangla does not mark present perfect in the same way as English, so students often default to present simple or past simple where present perfect is needed — drill the "changes/introductions linked to now" meaning explicitly with a timeline diagram if possible.
  • Passive voice awareness: Sections 6B ask students to notice when the passive is needed (e.g. "many cell phone towers have been built" — towers don't build themselves). This is a common source of errors; encourage students to ask "who or what performs the action?" before choosing active or passive.
  • 'while' + V-ing (Section 7): Remind students that dropping the subject and 'be' after 'while' (e.g. "while driving" not "while he is driving") is a mark of more advanced, natural academic style — but only works when the subject of both clauses is the same.
  • Reference words (Section 8): The 8B exercise is a strong diagnostic for overall writing quality — repetition of nouns is one of the most common Band 5-6 ceiling issues in Bangladeshi learners' essays. Encourage students to read their own writing aloud and notice repeated words themselves.
  • Conclusions (Section 9): Many students write conclusions that are too long or introduce new arguments. Reinforce: 1-2 sentences only, no new ideas, just summary + opinion.
  • Mobile phone use while driving is a matter of protecting life (hifz al-nafs), a core objective in Islamic ethics. Encourage students to reflect that clear, evidence-based writing about road safety is also a form of benefiting others (naf' al-nas) through honest communication.
