Model Text 1 — পড়ো ও বিশ্লেষণ করো

Essay Topic:

In the world today, there is a problem with food production. As a result, some people do not have enough to eat. Suggest some ways that farmers can solve this problem.

Model 1 — Two Solution Paragraphs

One way to increase food production is for people to eat grain such as barley, oats, or maize instead of feeding it to animals. More food is produced when farmers grow crops instead of animals on their land. While animals constantly need a lot of grass or grain, water, and space in which to move, crops need only water and fertiliser. Also, it costs money to turn the grain into meat, by feeding it to animals and then killing the animals. Therefore, the food yield per unit of land is far greater with crops than with animals.

Another way to increase food production is by improving the technology that farmers use. Firstly, better methods for irrigating land need to be developed. In countries which have little or no rain, such as parts of Africa, improved irrigation could lead to a big increase in food production. Secondly, if better seeds are developed, this could lead to stronger and healthier plants, which would provide more food yield per unit of land.

Grammar Focus 1 | Passive Voice | Passive Voice শেখো
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট

Passive voice ব্যবহার করি যখন আমরা জানি না কে কাজটি করছে, বা সেটা গুরুত্বপূর্ণ নয়। গুরুত্বপূর্ণ হলো কাজটি নিজে।

Active: Someone produces grain.
Passive: Grain is produced. (We don't care who produces it.)

Structure:
  • Present passive: Subject + is/are + past participle → Grain is produced. / Crops are grown.
  • Modal passive: Subject + modal + be + past participle → Food can be produced. / Seeds should be developed.
  • Need to passive: Subject + need(s) to be + past participle → Better methods need to be developed.
Key rule: After modal verbs (can, should, could, will, may, might), ALWAYS use 'be' + past participle — never 'is/are'.

Correct: Food can be produced. / Seeds should be developed.

Wrong: Food can is produced. / Seeds should are developed.

Grammar Focus 2 | Gerunds | Gerund শেখো
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট

Gerund হলো verb-এর -ing form যা noun হিসেবে ব্যবহার হয়।

As subject:

Growing crops can be very productive for small farmers. (Growing = subject of sentence)

After prepositions:

We can increase food production by improving our technology. (by + improving = gerund)

Common prepositions before gerunds:
  • for: responsible for supporting / concentrate on growing / methods for irrigating
  • by: increase production by improving technology / by educating people
  • on: concentrate on growing crops / focus on farming

Language Focus 2 | Linking Solution Paragraphs | 'One way' / 'Another way'
Solutions Essay Linking Words | সমাধান paragraph-এর linking words

IELTS Task 2-এ solutions essay লেখার সময় body paragraphs একটি নির্দিষ্ট ভাষায় শুরু করতে হয়:

  • Paragraph 1: One way to [solve the problem] is to/by/for...
  • Paragraph 2: Another way to [solve the problem] is to/by/for... OR Another way is by...
Pattern summary:
  • One way to + [verb phrase] + is to/by/for + [solution].
  • Another way (to + [verb phrase]) is to/by/for + [solution].

  6. Firstly: 'Firstly, the packaging on healthy foods should be marked to show that it is friendly…' Secondly: 'Secondly, the government could help by making healthy food cheaper…'
Teacher Notes  |  Unit 9 — Food: Teaching Tips
  • This unit introduces TWO new grammar points simultaneously — passive voice and gerunds. Teach them one at a time. Passive voice first (Section 6), then gerunds (Section 7). Avoid combining them until Section 7C.
  • Section 3 (Warm-Up): Q4 (passive voice definition) is a diagnostic question. If most students cannot write a basic passive sentence, spend extra time on Section 6 before moving on.
  • Section 4A (Model 1 Analysis): After students answer independently, go through the answers as a class. Highlight: (1) topic sentences both start with 'One way...' / 'Another way...'; (2) passive verbs — underline them; (3) gerunds — circle them.
  • Section 6 (Passive Voice): The most common error is confusing modal passive: students write 'seeds should developed' — remind them it must be 'should BE developed'. Write the structure on the board and have students refer to it throughout.
  • Section 6C (Active or Passive): This is the most cognitively demanding exercise. Remind students of the decision rule: if we know AND care who does the action → active. If we don't know or don't care who does it → passive.
  • Section 7 (Gerunds): Do NOT teach gerunds as 'verbs used as nouns' — this confuses students. Teach: 'some verbs act like nouns — they can be subjects or come after prepositions.' Use concrete examples.
  • Section 7B (Gerunds + Prepositions): Items c, d, e, g, h are gerunds used as subjects (no preposition). Items a, b, f use prepositions. Students often add an unnecessary preposition to subject gerunds.
  • Section 8 (While/Whereas): Remind students these can appear at the beginning OR middle of a sentence. The key is that the CONTRAST is real. Exercise 8B requires students to infer the contrast from notes — this is challenging but important.
  • Section 10 (Guided Writing): Before students attempt the gap-fill, ask them to re-read Model 1 and look for patterns in how gerunds and passives are combined. The gap-fill should reflect those patterns.
  • Section 11 (Writing Practice): Insist on the planning table being completed BEFORE writing. Students who plan first write more coherently. Mark primarily on: correct passive forms, correct gerund use, clear 'One way/Another way' structure.
