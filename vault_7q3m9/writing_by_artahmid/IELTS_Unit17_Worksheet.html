শেখার লক্ষ্য | Learning Objectives

এই worksheet শেষ করলে তুমি পারবে —

  • Read and interpret complex data tables showing changes over time.
  • Use 12 key vocabulary words for describing population and social changes.
  • Describe the magnitude of change using prepositional phrases: from...to..., by..., by more than...
  • Use non-defining relative clauses (who/which, with commas) to add extra data information.
  • Use 'although' and 'even though' to contrast surprising or unexpected information.
  • Write a full Task 1 paragraph describing a complex data table.

Model 1 — Population of Atlantis City: Changes in Ethnic Groups

The table shows the changes in ethnic population groups in Atlantis City over a twenty-year period. Overall, the city population grew significantly and all ethnic groups grew in size, but the percentages of each group changed considerably.

There was a massive reduction in the percentage of Caucasians, which decreased by about one third, from 68% in 1988 to 59% in 1998 and 47% in 2008, although their numbers increased from 60,000 to 72,000.

The Indian population stayed relatively stable for the first decade and then more than trebled from 2,500 to 8,500, in the decade from 1998 to 2008, even though it remained the smallest group in the city.

The Polynesians and South-East Asians had a similar growth pattern, increasing from 18% and 12% respectively in 1988 to 24% and 23% of the population in 2008, although the South-East Asian population, which grew from 11,000 in 1988 to 35,000 in 2008, had a greater percentage increase.

Essential Grammar | Prepositional Phrases for Change
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট

কত পরিবর্তন হয়েছে তা বলতে আমরা prepositional phrases ব্যবহার করি। এটি আমাদের data-কে নির্দিষ্ট figures দিয়ে statements support করতে সাহায্য করে।

  • from...to... → শুরু ও শেষ মান বলতে। Ex: from 68% to 47%
  • by... → পরিবর্তনের পরিমাণ বলতে। Ex: decreased by about one third
  • by more than... → "বেশি" বোঝাতে। Ex: increased by more than 100 per cent
  • at... → একটি নির্দিষ্ট মানে স্থির থাকলে। Ex: remained stable at 2 per cent

Language Focus 1 | Non-defining Relative Clauses | Non-defining Relative Clause
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট

Non-defining relative clause কী? এই ধরনের clause একটি noun সম্পর্কে অতিরিক্ত তথ্য দেয়। এটি সবসময় comma দিয়ে আলাদা করা হয়।

  • who → মানুষ বা group-এর জন্য। Ex: The Indians, who made up one of the smallest groups, were recent arrivals.
  • which → জিনিস বা concept-এর জন্য। Ex: ...the percentage of Caucasians, which decreased by about one third...

মূল নিয়ম: Non-defining clause সবসময় সেই noun-এর ঠিক পরে আসে যেটি সম্পর্কে বলা হচ্ছে। Comma (,) এবং comma (,) দিয়ে clause-টি আলাদা করতে হয়।

Language Focus 2 | 'although', 'even though', 'though'
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট

'although', 'even though', 'though' — তিনটির অর্থ একই। কোনো কিছু অপ্রত্যাশিত বা বিপরীত তথ্য বোঝাতে ব্যবহার হয়। এগুলো বাক্যের শুরুতে বা মাঝে আসতে পারে।

  • Although/Even though + clause, + main clause.
  • Main clause + although/even though + clause.
  • উদাহরণ: ...their numbers increased from 60,000 to 72,000, although their percentage decreased significantly.
  • উদাহরণ: Even though the Indian population trebled, it remained the smallest group.

সাবধান: 'although'/'even though'-এর সাথে 'but' ব্যবহার করা যাবে না। এবং 'even' (without 'though') এর ভিন্ন অর্থ আছে।

Model 2 — Workforce of Atlantis City, aged 18–65

The table shows the changes in the employment status of the population of Atlantic City over two decades. Overall, the percentage of part-time workers nearly doubled in this period, while the number of unemployed people declined significantly.

The percentage of full-time workers stayed relatively stable over the two decades, at around 40%, although the total number increased from 36,000 in 1988 to 40,000 in 1998 and 65,000 in 2008.

There was a massive increase in the number of part-time workers, which rose from 19,000 to 59,000 and whose percentage increased steadily from 21% to 38% of the workforce.

In contrast, the proportion of the unemployed, who were the smallest group, reduced more than threefold from 11% to 3%, and their numbers also dropped in the decade from 1998 to 2008.

The percentage of students rose in 1998 and then declined dramatically from 33% to 18%, although the total number of students rose slightly from 24,000 in 1988 to 28,000 in 2008.

Teacher Notes

Unit 17 — Social Issues: Population: Teaching Tips & Notes

  • This worksheet is designed for IELTS Task 1 (complex table description). Students should be comfortable with basic pie chart and bar chart description (Units 1–2) before attempting this unit, as tables combine several data categories at once.
  • Section 4 (Table Reading): Spend extra time here — many students struggle to read a table with multiple sub-columns (Number and %). Model reading one row aloud first, tracing across the table with a finger or ruler.
  • Section 5B (Xanadu Text): This is a dense exercise combining word-form choice and vocabulary recall. Consider letting weaker students complete it in pairs.
  • Sections 7–9 (Grammar Focus): Prepositional phrases, non-defining relative clauses, and although/even though are all high-level grammar structures that examiners reward in Band 7+ scripts. Encourage students to use at least one of each in their final paragraph, but warn against overusing them, as forced or unnatural grammar can lower the Coherence and Cohesion score.
  • Guided Writing & Writing Practice: Remind students that describing a table well is about grouping and selecting the most important data — not listing every number. Encourage them to group similar categories together (e.g., full-time and part-time as "employed") rather than describing each column separately.
  • Every learner in this class, regardless of their current writing level, is a unique creation of Allah, deserving of patience and encouragement. Population and social data can sometimes carry sensitive comparisons between groups — remind students that describing statistics accurately in English is a language skill, and should never be used to make value judgements about any community.
