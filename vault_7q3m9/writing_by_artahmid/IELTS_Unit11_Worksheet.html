Essay Topic:

Some countries attempt to solve the problem of traffic congestion by limiting the use of cars in inner-city areas. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this approach to urban planning?

Model 1 — Introduction + Two Body Paragraphs
[Introduction]

In the past twenty years, the amount of traffic on the roads has greatly increased, and this has led to many problems with traffic jams and pollution in inner-city areas. Some countries have limited the use of cars in the inner city by charging fees or restricting the days on which people can drive into the city. This approach has some disadvantages but, on the whole, it is a good solution to the problems of inner-city traffic.

[Body Paragraph 1 — Disadvantages]

Restricting car traffic in the city can make it very difficult for some people to do their daily work. For example, moving quickly to meetings is very important for businessmen, and if they have no car, this could create problems for them. In addition, it is important for disabled people or for families with young children to access city services, and they should be able to drive their cars to the places where they need to go. Limiting the use of cars could create real inconvenience for some citizens.

[Body Paragraph 2 — Advantages]

However, there are many advantages to this idea. Firstly, it greatly reduces inner-city pollution, both by reducing the number of cars and by encouraging the use of public transport. This reduction in pollution benefits everyone who lives and works in the city. Secondly, it decreases traffic congestion and makes it easier to move around the city. Thus, businessmen may find it quicker to get to their meetings and to visit other locations either by taxi, by bus, or by rail transport.

Grammar Focus | Present Perfect Tense — Changes
Grammar Note | ব্যাকরণ নোট

Present perfect tense ব্যবহার করি যখন কোনো পরিবর্তন অতীতে শুরু হয়েছে এবং এখন পর্যন্ত চলছে বা এখন পর্যন্ত প্রভাব রেখেছে।

Form: have / has + past participle (verb 3rd form)
  • Present perfect (change): The amount of traffic HAS GREATLY INCREASED. (changed over time — still relevant now)
  • Present perfect (result): This HAS LED TO many problems with traffic jams. (result of a past change)
  • Present simple (fact): Traffic jams CAUSE pollution. (always true)
  • Present simple (opinion): This approach HAS some disadvantages. (writer's view — stated as fact)
Key signal words for present perfect:

in recent years / in the past [X] years / recently / has become / has grown / has increased / has led to

Language Focus | Writing an Introduction | Introduction কীভাবে লিখতে হয়
An introduction has TWO purposes | Introduction-এর দুটো উদ্দেশ্য

Purpose 1: To introduce the TOPIC of the essay. (Essay-এর বিষয় পরিচয় করাও।)

Purpose 2: To tell the reader HOW you will organise your ideas. (পাঠককে বলো essay কীভাবে সাজানো হবে।)

A good introduction for an advantages/disadvantages essay typically:

  • Gives background/context information using present perfect tense (changes that have happened).
  • Names the approach or solution being discussed.
  • Signals the essay structure — 'This approach has some disadvantages but, on the whole, it is a good solution…'

Model 2 — Complete Essay
[Introduction]

Recently, there has been a huge increase in the number of cars on the roads. This has caused many problems with traffic congestion in inner-city areas. Some cities have tried to solve this problem by building motorways underground or above street level. This approach has some advantages but, in general, it is not the best solution to the problems of inner-city traffic.

[Body Paragraph 1 — Advantages]

Elevated or underground motorway networks can reduce congestion by removing a lot of traffic from city streets. Thus, it is much easier to travel around the city and quicker to get from one place to another. In addition, these new networks do not need to follow existing streets, so they can take a more direct route from one place to another, and this also makes it easier to travel from place to place.

[Body Paragraph 2 — Disadvantages]

However, this approach has a lot of disadvantages. Firstly, it does nothing to reduce the number of cars in the city, and this means that it does not reduce pollution. In addition, it is difficult to build motorway networks in an existing city. Consequently, it takes a long time and creates a lot of congestion while the new motorways are being built. Finally, the cars that come into the city on these new networks have to come back to street level once they reach their destination. This means that there will still be a lot of cars in the city.

Section 8B — Introduction Ordering
  • Introduction A: 2 → 1 → 3 (General popularity → specific content concern → current situation/contrast)
  • Introduction B: 3 → 2 → 1 (Background change → current figure → consequence)
  • Introduction C: 2 → 1 → 3 (General statement → one option → other option/contrast)
Section 9 — Paragraph Ordering (Model 2)

Correct essay paragraph order: Introduction → Body Para 1 (Advantages) → Body Para 2 (Disadvantages)

  • Introduction (Block B): 3 → 1 → 4 → 2 (Recently… / This has caused… / Some cities… / This approach has…)
  • Body Para 1 — Advantages (Block A): 2 → 1 → 3 (Elevated networks can… / Thus, it is easier… / In addition, these networks…)
  • Body Para 2 — Disadvantages (Block C): 3 → 1 → 6 → 2 → 4 → 5 → (linked to) 4 (However… / Firstly… / In addition, it is difficult… / Consequently… / Finally… / This means…)

Note: Check sentence order against Model 2 text for exact ordering. The key logic: topic sentence first, then Firstly/In addition/Consequently/Finally signal the sequence.

Teacher Notes  |  Unit 11 — Transport: Teaching Tips
  • This is the first unit to explicitly teach the INTRODUCTION. Spend at least 10 minutes on Section 8 (introduction analysis and ordering) as this skill carries through all remaining units.
  • Section 3 (Warm-Up): Q2 (3 solutions to traffic congestion) can be done as a brief class brainstorm. Students often surprise themselves with how much they know. Write ideas on the board.
  • Section 4A (Model 1 Analysis): Highlight that Model 1 is THREE paragraphs — Introduction + Disadvantages + Advantages. This is NOT the only valid order, but it is the one the model uses. Discuss with students WHY disadvantages might come first.
  • Section 6 (Present Perfect): The key insight is: present perfect = a CHANGE that started in the past and is still relevant or still happening. The clearest test: can you add 'in recent years' or 'in the past X years'? If yes → probably present perfect.
  • Section 6B (Tense Fill): There are some borderline cases where both present perfect and present continuous/simple are acceptable. Accept these. What is NOT acceptable is using past simple ('increased') for recent ongoing trends.
  • Section 7 (Adjective Constructions): Students often confuse Pattern 1 and Pattern 2. Drill the key difference: Pattern 1 starts with 'It'; Pattern 2 starts with a gerund or noun subject.
